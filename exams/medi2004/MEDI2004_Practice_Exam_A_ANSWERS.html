PART B — Short-answer model answers & marking guide (50 marks)

Model answers are indicative; award marks for equivalent correct pharmacology. Suggested mark allocation shown per point.

SAQ 1 — Enalapril / prodrugs / bioavailability (10 marks)

A) (4)
A prodrug is an inactive (or much less active) compound that must be biotransformed in the body to release the active drug. Enalapril is hydrolysed by hepatic esterases to its active metabolite enalaprilat. Enalaprilat inhibits angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), so less angiotensin I is converted to angiotensin II → vasodilation and reduced aldosterone-driven Na⁺/water retention → lower blood pressure.
1 = prodrug defined · 1 = activated by hepatic esterases · 1 = names enalaprilat · 1 = ACE inhibition → ↓angiotensin II → ↓BP.
B) (3)
Increases bioavailability. The "+inhibitor" curve shows a higher Cmax and a larger area under the curve (AUC) than "drug alone." Because the amount reaching the systemic circulation is proportional to AUC, the larger AUC indicates greater bioavailability (the inhibitor blocks first-pass metabolism, so more drug survives).
1 = states increase · 1 = reads larger AUC/Cmax from graph · 1 = links AUC to bioavailability / first-pass.
C) (3)
Enalapril must be activated by hepatic esterases. In severe hepatic impairment, this conversion is reduced/slower → less enalaprilat is formed → less ACE inhibition → reduced (or delayed) antihypertensive effect.
1 = reduced hepatic activation · 1 = less active enalaprilat · 1 = blunted therapeutic effect.

SAQ 2 — Propranolol / salbutamol / receptor pharmacology (10 marks)

A) (3)
Affinity = how strongly a drug binds its receptor. Efficacy = the ability of a bound drug to activate the receptor and produce a response. Salbutamol has efficacy at β2 (it is an agonist — binds and activates); propranolol has affinity but essentially zero efficacy (antagonist).
1 = affinity · 1 = efficacy · 1 = salbutamol has efficacy, with reason.
B) (3)
β2-adrenoceptors on bronchial smooth muscle mediate bronchodilation (Gs → cAMP → relaxation). Propranolol is a non-selective β-antagonist, so it blocks β2 and prevents endogenous adrenaline from producing bronchodilation. Loss of bronchodilator tone → unopposed bronchoconstriction/wheeze, dangerous in asthma.
1 = β2 → bronchodilation · 1 = propranolol blocks β2 (non-selective) · 1 = loss of dilation → bronchoconstriction.
C) (2)
Competitive antagonist binds reversibly at the same (orthosteric) site; block is surmountable by more agonist (parallel rightward shift, Emax preserved). Irreversible antagonist binds covalently/very tightly; reduces Emax and is insurmountable. Propranolol is a competitive (reversible) antagonist.
1 = correct distinction · 1 = propranolol = competitive.
D) (1)
A cell-surface receptor — specifically a G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR).
E) (1)
cAMP (cyclic AMP).

SAQ 4 — β-adrenergic signalling & drug design (19 marks)

A) (4 — 1 each)
Receptor: β1-adrenoceptor · Enzyme: adenylyl cyclase (or PDE) · Ion channel: L-type Ca²⁺ channel · Transporter: NET (noradrenaline transporter).
B) (4)
NA binds β1 (GPCR) → activates Gαs (GDP→GTP) → Gαs stimulates adenylyl cyclase → ↑cAMP → activates PKA → PKA phosphorylates L-type Ca²⁺ channels → ↑Ca²⁺ influx → stronger excitation–contraction coupling → ↑contractility.
1 = Gαs activation · 1 = AC → cAMP · 1 = PKA · 1 = Ca-channel phosphorylation → ↑Ca → ↑contractility.
C) (3)
Target: β1-adrenoceptor. Type: antagonist (beta-blocker, e.g., metoprolol/bisoprolol). Mechanism: competitively blocks NA/adrenaline at β1 → ↓Gαs→AC→cAMP→PKA → ↓Ca²⁺ influx → ↓heart rate and contractility (↓cardiac workload & O₂ demand).
1 = β1 target · 1 = antagonist · 1 = mechanism to ↓rate/contractility.
D) (2)
Increases contractility. PDE normally degrades cAMP → AMP; inhibiting PDE lets cAMP accumulate → more PKA → more Ca²⁺ influx → stronger contraction.
1 = ↑contractility · 1 = via ↑cAMP (blocked breakdown).
E) (2)
NET is a transporter. A NET inhibitor blocks reuptake of noradrenaline into the nerve terminal → NA accumulates in the synapse → prolonged/enhanced adrenergic signalling.
1 = transporter · 1 = ↑synaptic NA via blocked reuptake.
F) (4)
Chronically high cAMP → sustained PKA → persistently raised intracellular Ca²⁺. Consequences: Ca²⁺ overload → triggered arrhythmias (after-depolarisations); ↑heart rate/contractility → ↑myocardial O₂ demand in an already failing, energy-starved heart; maladaptive remodelling/apoptosis and β-receptor desensitisation. Net effect: symptom relief short-term but increased arrhythmia and mortality long-term.
Award up to 4 across: Ca overload/arrhythmia · ↑O₂ demand/workload · remodelling/apoptosis or desensitisation · coherent link to ↑mortality.
