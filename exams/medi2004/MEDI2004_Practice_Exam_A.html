PART A: MULTIPLE-CHOICE QUESTIONS (50 MARKS)
Answer ALL questions. Choose the single best answer for each question.
  1. A textbook defines one branch of pharmacology as "the study of the biochemical and physiological effects of drugs and their mechanisms of action." This describes:
    • A. pharmacodynamics
    • B. pharmacogenomics
    • C. pharmacokinetics
    • D. pharmacovigilance
  2. Atorvastatin is marketed under the brand name Lipitor. The name "atorvastatin" is the drug's:
    • A. brand (proprietary) name
    • B. chemical name
    • C. generic (non-proprietary) name
    • D. trade name
  3. A drug given orally reaches the systemic circulation at 40% of the amount achieved by an equivalent intravenous dose. The oral bioavailability (F) of this drug is:
    • A. 0.4
    • B. 0.6
    • C. 2.5
    • D. not determinable without the half-life
  4. Glyceryl trinitrate is given sublingually rather than swallowed. Sublingual delivery is used chiefly because it avoids extensive first-pass metabolism in the:
    • A. kidney
    • B. liver
    • C. lung
    • D. stomach wall
  5. Aspirin is a weak acid (pKa ≈ 3.5). In the acidic stomach lumen (pH ≈ 2), aspirin is predominantly:
    • A. ionised, which favours absorption
    • B. ionised, which opposes absorption
    • C. unionised, which favours absorption
    • D. unionised, which opposes absorption
  6. In a phenobarbital (a weak acid) overdose, sodium bicarbonate is given to alkalinise the urine. This increases elimination because, in alkaline urine, the drug becomes more:
    • A. ionised and is trapped in the tubule
    • B. ionised and is reabsorbed
    • C. unionised and is trapped in the tubule
    • D. unionised and is reabsorbed
  7. A drug has a volume of distribution of 700 L in a 70 kg adult. This value is best interpreted as the drug being:
    • A. confined to the plasma
    • B. extensively distributed into tissues
    • C. highly ionised at physiological pH
    • D. poorly absorbed from the gut
  8. Two highly protein-bound drugs are co-administered and one displaces the other from albumin. The immediate effect on the displaced drug is an increase in its:
    • A. albumin-bound fraction
    • B. free (unbound) fraction
    • C. rate of absorption
    • D. volume of distribution
  9. Most small, lipophilic drug molecules cross cell membranes primarily by:
    • A. active transport
    • B. endocytosis
    • C. facilitated diffusion
    • D. passive diffusion
  10. Rifampicin is a potent inducer of CYP3A4. When co-administered with a CYP3A4-substrate drug, the substrate's plasma concentration is most likely to:
    • A. decrease, risking therapeutic failure
    • B. decrease, risking toxicity
    • C. increase, risking therapeutic failure
    • D. increase, risking toxicity
  11. Grapefruit juice inhibits intestinal CYP3A4. Taken with the CYP3A4 substrate felodipine, grapefruit juice increases felodipine's:
    • A. systemic clearance
    • B. first-pass metabolism
    • C. oral bioavailability
    • D. volume of distribution
  12. Which statement is CORRECT for a drug that follows first-order elimination kinetics?
    • A. a constant amount is eliminated per unit time
    • B. a constant fraction is eliminated per unit time
    • C. clearance falls as concentration rises
    • D. half-life lengthens as the dose increases
  13. A drug with an elimination half-life of 8 hours is given repeatedly at a fixed interval with no loading dose. Steady state will be reached in approximately:
    • A. 8 hours
    • B. 16 hours
    • C. 40 hours
    • D. 80 hours
  14. A drug is eliminated almost entirely unchanged by the kidneys. In a patient with significant renal impairment, the most appropriate action is to:
    • A. increase the dose to overcome poor excretion
    • B. reduce the dose or lengthen the interval
    • C. switch the patient to the oral route
    • D. make no change, as the liver compensates
  15. A drug is conjugated in the liver, excreted in bile, deconjugated by gut bacteria, then reabsorbed. This enterohepatic recycling tends to:
    • A. have no effect on drug exposure
    • B. prolong the drug's duration of action
    • C. abolish oral bioavailability
    • D. increase renal first-pass metabolism
  16. A drug binds its receptor with high affinity but produces no response even when all receptors are occupied. This drug has:
    • A. high affinity and high efficacy
    • B. high affinity and zero efficacy
    • C. low affinity and high efficacy
    • D. low affinity and zero efficacy
  17. Buprenorphine produces a submaximal effect even at full receptor occupancy. Given together with the full agonist morphine, buprenorphine can behave as a functional:
    • A. antagonist
    • B. inverse agonist
    • C. irreversible antagonist
    • D. positive allosteric modulator
  18. Adding a reversible competitive antagonist to an agonist shifts the agonist's concentration–response curve:
    • A. downward, lowering Emax
    • B. leftward, lowering EC50
    • C. rightward in parallel, with Emax unchanged
    • D. rightward, with Emax lowered
  19. The reduction in maximal agonist response caused by a high concentration of an irreversible antagonist canNOT be reversed by:
    • A. adding a partial agonist
    • B. increasing the agonist concentration
    • C. lowering the assay temperature
    • D. waiting for receptor turnover
  20. An inverse agonist can only be distinguished from a neutral antagonist when the receptor system:
    • A. contains spare receptors
    • B. shows constitutive activity
    • C. is fully desensitised
    • D. is saturated with a full agonist
  21. Drug X has an EC50 of 1 nM and drug Y an EC50 of 100 nM at the same receptor. Compared with drug Y, drug X is:
    • A. 100× less potent
    • B. 100× more potent
    • C. equally potent
    • D. necessarily more efficacious
  22. Warfarin exerts its anticoagulant effect by inhibiting the enzyme vitamin K epoxide reductase. Warfarin's molecular drug target is therefore:
    • A. an enzyme
    • B. an ion channel
    • C. a cell-surface receptor
    • D. a membrane transporter
  23. A Gαs-coupled receptor is activated by its agonist. The expected downstream change is:
    • A. decreased adenylyl cyclase and decreased cAMP
    • B. increased adenylyl cyclase and increased cAMP
    • C. increased phospholipase C and increased IP3
    • D. direct opening of a ligand-gated channel
  24. Activation of a Gαq-coupled receptor raises the intracellular second messengers:
    • A. cAMP and protein kinase A
    • B. cGMP and nitric oxide
    • C. IP3 and diacylglycerol (DAG)
    • D. potassium and chloride ions
  25. Steroid and thyroid hormones act on intracellular (nuclear) receptors that alter gene transcription. Compared with ligand-gated ion channels, their onset of effect is:
    • A. faster, on a millisecond scale
    • B. faster, because transcription is rapid
    • C. slower, on a scale of hours
    • D. slower, because they open ion pores
  26. Insulin acts on a receptor tyrosine kinase. Ligand binding to this receptor class typically triggers:
    • A. GDP–GTP exchange on a G protein
    • B. direct opening of an ion pore
    • C. receptor dimerisation and autophosphorylation
    • D. release of the receptor from a nuclear chaperone
  27. Neostigmine inhibits acetylcholinesterase. The immediate consequence at the synapse is:
    • A. less acetylcholine and a weaker cholinergic effect
    • B. more acetylcholine and a stronger cholinergic effect
    • C. faster breakdown of acetylcholine
    • D. blockade of postsynaptic muscarinic receptors
  28. Sertraline, an SSRI, blocks the serotonin transporter (SERT). By inhibiting this transporter, sertraline:
    • A. speeds serotonin breakdown in the cleft
    • B. raises serotonin concentration in the cleft
    • C. prevents serotonin from being released
    • D. reduces the number of serotonin receptors
  29. Lidocaine produces local anaesthesia by blocking:
    • A. calcium channels at the nerve terminal
    • B. ligand-gated sodium channels
    • C. potassium channels along the axon
    • D. voltage-gated sodium channels
  30. Phenytoin binds voltage-gated sodium channels preferentially in their inactivated state. This "use-dependence" means it most strongly suppresses:
    • A. all neurons equally
    • B. rapidly and repetitively firing neurons
    • C. resting, silent neurons
    • D. skeletal muscle fibres only
  31. Amlodipine, a dihydropyridine, lowers blood pressure mainly by blocking L-type calcium channels in:
    • A. cardiac pacemaker cells
    • B. skeletal muscle
    • C. central neurons
    • D. vascular smooth muscle
  32. Cystic fibrosis arises from dysfunction of the CFTR protein. CFTR is a:
    • A. chloride channel
    • B. potassium channel
    • C. sodium–potassium pump
    • D. voltage-gated calcium channel
  33. A dose-dependent, predictable adverse reaction that is an exaggeration of the drug's known pharmacology is classified as a:
    • A. Type A (augmented) reaction
    • B. Type B (bizarre) reaction
    • C. Type C (chronic) reaction
    • D. Type D (delayed) reaction
  34. Which of the following is best classified as a Type B (idiosyncratic, unpredictable) adverse drug reaction?
    • A. bleeding with warfarin
    • B. bradycardia with a beta-blocker
    • C. anaphylaxis to penicillin
    • D. constipation with an opioid
  35. In paracetamol overdose, hepatotoxicity develops because the reactive metabolite NAPQI accumulates once:
    • A. glucuronidation is enhanced
    • B. glutathione stores are depleted
    • C. renal excretion is increased
    • D. CYP2E1 is fully inhibited
  36. The antidote N-acetylcysteine is effective in paracetamol overdose because it:
    • A. inhibits CYP enzymes directly
    • B. replenishes hepatic glutathione
    • C. binds paracetamol in the gut lumen
    • D. alkalinises the urine to trap NAPQI
  37. A drug with a narrow therapeutic index, such as digoxin, generally requires:
    • A. no monitoring, as the dose is fixed
    • B. therapeutic drug monitoring of plasma levels
    • C. only a single loading dose
    • D. administration by the IV route only
  38. Two drugs both prolong the QT interval and, when combined, additively increase arrhythmia risk without altering each other's plasma levels. This is a:
    • A. pharmaceutical (in-vitro) interaction
    • B. pharmacodynamic interaction
    • C. pharmacokinetic absorption interaction
    • D. pharmacokinetic metabolism interaction
  39. Codeine is a prodrug converted by CYP2D6 to morphine. In a CYP2D6 poor metaboliser, standard-dose codeine will most likely produce:
    • A. dangerous morphine toxicity
    • B. inadequate analgesia
    • C. normal analgesia
    • D. a type I hypersensitivity reaction
  40. A CYP2D6 ultrarapid metaboliser given standard-dose codeine is at increased risk of:
    • A. inadequate analgesia
    • B. opioid toxicity from excess morphine
    • C. no change relative to normal metabolisers
    • D. reduced codeine absorption
  41. Clopidogrel is a prodrug requiring CYP2C19 for activation. A patient with a loss-of-function CYP2C19 variant will most likely have:
    • A. a stronger antiplatelet effect and bleeding
    • B. a weaker antiplatelet effect and higher clot risk
    • C. no change in antiplatelet effect
    • D. markedly faster clopidogrel clearance
  42. Adalimumab is a monoclonal antibody. Because it is a large protein, it:
    • A. has high oral bioavailability
    • B. must be given by injection, not orally
    • C. readily crosses the blood–brain barrier
    • D. is cleared mainly by CYP3A4
  43. A biosimilar is best described as a biological medicine that is:
    • A. chemically identical to the reference biologic
    • B. a small-molecule generic of the biologic
    • C. highly similar to an approved reference biologic
    • D. an inactive placebo used in trials
  44. Designing a molecule to fit the known three-dimensional structure of a target enzyme's active site is an example of:
    • A. high-throughput random screening
    • B. natural-product screening
    • C. rational (structure-based) drug design
    • D. serendipitous discovery
  45. In Australia, a Schedule 8 (S8) medicine is a:
    • A. general (unscheduled) sales item
    • B. pharmacist-only (S3) medicine
    • C. prescription-only (S4) medicine
    • D. controlled drug with strict record-keeping
  46. A higher-risk medicine individually evaluated by the TGA for quality, safety and efficacy carries which marking on its label?
    • A. AUST L (listed)
    • B. AUST R (registered)
    • C. AUST T (traditional)
    • D. no marking is required
  47. TGA registration and PBS listing differ in that:
    • A. both decide government subsidy
    • B. the PBS decides subsidy; the TGA decides market approval
    • C. the TGA sets the price; the PBS approves safety
    • D. they are two names for the same process
  48. The primary aim of a Phase I clinical trial is to assess a drug's:
    • A. cost-effectiveness versus standard care
    • B. efficacy in a large patient population
    • C. rare long-term adverse effects
    • D. safety, tolerability and pharmacokinetics
  49. In a double-blind randomised controlled trial, neither participants nor investigators know the treatment allocation. The main purpose of this design is to:
    • A. increase the study's sample size
    • B. reduce bias from expectation and observation
    • C. shorten the overall trial duration
    • D. remove the need for a control group
  50. Rare adverse drug reactions that emerge only after a medicine is widely marketed are most likely to be detected during:
    • A. preclinical (animal) testing
    • B. Phase I first-in-human trials
    • C. Phase II efficacy trials
    • D. Phase IV post-marketing surveillance
